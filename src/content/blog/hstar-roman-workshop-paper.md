I'm pleased to share that I've had a workshop paper accepted at the **Human State-Aware Robotics (H-STAR): From Multimodal Data to Human-Adaptive Behavior in HRI** workshop at **RO-MAN 2026** in Kitakyushu, Japan.

The paper, *What Are We Measuring? Bonding, Trust, and the Evaluation of Human–Robot Relationships*, takes aim at a habit that has quietly become standard in HRI: using **trust**, usually captured with a self-report questionnaire, as a stand-in for the quality of a human–robot relationship as a whole.

In this short paper, I argue this is a category error. Trust is a cognitive appraisal of whether a robot will perform reliably: forward-looking, quick to update, and fragile, such that a single salient failure can undo it. **Social bonding** is something else i.e. an affiliative social connection that accumulates slowly through repeated interaction, and shows up in largely involuntary, bodily (physiological and biobehavioural) signals. Trust and bonding *can* track, and they often do, but they can also come apart.

So, when we treat bond and trust as two independent dimensions rather than one, we get a 2D space of relationship states, which I simply call the *bond × trust* space. The paper breaks this space down into four "regions" of relationship types, where low bond and low trust = **avoidance**, low bond and high trust = **functional**, high bond and low trust = **dependence**, and high bond, high trust = **symbiosis**. I discuss some of the practical and ethical considerations of thinking about bonding and trust in this way, and look forward to talking about it more at RO-MAN 2026.
